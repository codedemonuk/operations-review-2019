#### Safer Minds

2 years £16,000 (£8k per year)

Towards providing a Community Support Worker in Hertsmere. Match funding has been secured from Clarion Housing, Hertsmere Borough Council, Hertsmere Community Safety Partnership, Herts Mind Network, St Albans & Hertsmere Women’s Refuge and Health & Wellbeing Partnership.
At least one in four Hertfordshire residents will experience mental health problems at some point in their lives - often not diagnosed or requiring specialist services according to the Hertfordshire Mental Health Strategy. At present only 15% of these are being targeted with access to a mental health service. This means that 85% of them may have no structured support.
The PCC acknowledges in ‘Everybody’s Business’ that the number of incidents attended by police that are linked to mental health has doubled and its estimated that responding to mental health challenges’ takes up 40% of police time

This post aims to address some of these issues and support people with drug and alcohol difficulties and mental ill-health who may have been victims of crime or have an offending history, particularly when they are coming into repeat contact with Police Services, Tenancy Sustainment Services, Housing and Homelessness Services and Environmental Health Services.
The post was filled in November and is hosted by Herts Mind Network (HMN) based at their Wellbeing Centre in Leeming Road, Borehamwood; with outreach support in HMN’s Potters Bar base and at the St Albans & Hertsmere Women’s Refuge.
To date our worker has had 25 referrals and is actively working with 14 clients.

#### Be Fearless Against Abuse (in partnership with Crime Stoppers)

£29,613 over two years

To fund 2 conferences in years 1 and 2 and a dedicated officer in year 2.

In May Hertsmere hosted the Fearless Training Day - Fearless partnered with Enough Abuse to educate and raise awareness of child abuse.
One hundred Teachers and professionals working with young people were afforded the opportunity to hear about the Fearless programme.
The topics covered included:

- Child Sexual Exploitation
- Fearless and its key messages
- Working with young people and talking about crime
- Cyber bullying
- The ‘Thrive Programme.

Fearless comprises a dedicated curriculum package and website where young people can access non-judgmental information and advice about crime and criminality. Thus, helping young people to make appropriate choices when faced with difficult decisions.
The Fearless officer will engage with schools primarily, working with teachers, children and youth, providing education on CSE foremost, but also other priority crime types for each individual school. Once training has been completed, across teachers/staff, parents and pupils, the Fearless Officer will then leave the school with a resource pack, including lesson plans, for continued delivery.

The Fearless service enables young people to report crime safely and 100% anonymously.
Young people can do this by completing a secure online form via our website, www.fearless.org, or by calling the Crimestoppers 0800 555 111 number.
The website also offers information and advice about a range of crimes.

### PCC Funding Bids Made to the Community Safety Fund 2019 20

Note:

A number bids have been submitted:

#### Dual Diagnosis Specialist – Community Support Service

3 years - £99,500

To provide Three Rivers and Hertsmere CSP’s with a dual diagnosis specialist worker with the pre-existing Community Support Service (CSS) teams procured through Herts Mind Network. This role aims to engage vulnerable residents misusing drugs and alcohol who are also experiencing mental ill health or have mental wellbeing needs when they are coming into contact with Drug and Alcohol, Police, Housing and Homelessness Services and Community Safety Services.
The service will be available to residents aged 18+ living in Three Rivers, Hertsmere or residents who are tenants of Watford Community Housing Trust.
The service aims to reduce the risk and vulnerability of exploitation, homelessness and criminality of those misusing drugs or alcohol in the area whilst simultaneously increase the mental wellbeing of people in Three Rivers and Hertsmere, and increase access to support for both needs.

#### SOS – Drawing the Lines – a county wide intervention for serious violence

1 year £140,000

This project seeks to establish four additional SOS Project Workers from St Giles’ Trust to join the existing worker based in Broxbourne. As identified in the Hertfordshire Strategic Needs Assessment a whole-system county-wide approach to reducing violent crime is the recommended model, this proposal takes account of this key finding and seeks to embed these project workers within the District / Borough Councils with the highest risk of serious violence.

The four additional workers will enable a county-wide intervention which innovates a solution combining the efforts of all ten Community Safety Partnerships (CSP) alongside the County Partnerships to revolutionise the way Hertfordshire addresses violence and continue to work with already embedded local services .

By intervening with the young people most at risk of criminal exploitation we will seek to eradicate the foot soldiers of serious and organised crime gangs and establish an environment in Hertfordshire that is resilient to county lines and organised crime.

The four workers will enable all District / Borough Councils to have access to an intervention which will seek to offer local schools the opportunity to refer high risk young people into an intensive support service, which is integrated with the anti-social behaviour groups operating in each CSP, the Family Safeguarding Teams, Families First Triage Teams, Youth Justice Teams, Domestic Abuse Services, the Channel Panel, and Adult Social Care / HPFT Mental Health Teams.

This project; supported by a Project Manager, will provide an action based development process, that recognises the individual priorities of each CSP, supports integrated learning and the development of best practice, supported by HCC’s Public Health Intelligence Unit and County Community Safety Unit.

#### Better Choices expansion - “Early Intervention” for Youth Violent Crime

3 years -  £54,000

Three Rivers, Watford and Hertsmere CSP’s are working in partnership to develop a Youth Violent Crime Reduction Project in line with the Hertfordshire Serious Violence Strategy, to intervene and prevent young people from becoming victims or perpetrators of violent crime and associated behaviours. This project aims to identify and share information amongst a multi-agency panel regarding young people of concern, as either perpetrators of crime, victims of exploitation or those at risk of either behaviours.
Part of this project will involve Chessbrook Education Support Centre (a registered Pupil Referral Unit) chairing a Youth Violent Crime Reduction Panel, with multi-agency involvement to risk assess, action plan, track the intervention and progress of young people referred in to the Panel.
The three CSP’s have identified a gap in provision for those needing “early intervention”. The funding received through this bid will expand the current “Better Choices” project run by YC Hertfordshire, to allow for existing YC Hertfordshire staff with dedicated capacity to work individually with at risk young people and their families (if required).
This service aims to deliver a targeted and proactive approach to early intervention. The workers would provide young people aged 11-18 with specialist one to one support and advice on action planning, emotional wellbeing, lifestyle choices, protective behaviours and relationships, which may enable them to access to additional diversionary activities provided through YC Hertfordshire or the Panel.
We aim to provide 450 hours of support per year to young people referred to the service. This is instead of having a fixed number of young people to support, to allow for flexibility in duration of intervention dependant on the young person’s level of need or the needs of their family. Flexibility will enable YC Hertfordshire to provide a client-focussed service.

#### Hertfordshire Modern Slavery Partnership Coordinator

1 year - £32,000

After an initial awareness-raising conference in March 2017, the Police and Crime Commissioner for Hertfordshire, Shiva Foundation, Hertfordshire County Council and Hertfordshire Constabulary set up the Hertfordshire Modern Slavery Partnership. The purpose of the Partnership being to increase joint-efforts across the county in raising awareness of modern slavery and human trafficking, sharing information across agencies, and proactively working together to address this issue. A strategic Steering Group leads the partnership and is accountable to an agreed strategy and related action plan.
There are four sub-groups working on (1) tactical and operational response, (2) support for victims, (3) partnerships and (4) health.

Since the inception of the Partnership Shiva Foundation has been acting as its independent coordinator. It was agreed that due to identified gaps in role performance that Shiva Foundation could not address within its current capacity (i.e. being an external organisation), that a suitable replacement would step in to help drive forward the Partnership’s strategy and action plan. It is important that this role be independent, from neutral territory (rather than staffed by any one agency), therefore the role will be partially funded and line-managed by Shiva Foundation.

The proposal, as agreed by the Partnership Steering Group, is for one full-time Coordinator to be recruited for one financial year from April 2019 to March 2020.

The PCC’s office will be undertaking an initial assessment of all applications during January; undertaking research and seeking clarification, where necessary.  It is hoped that final decisions will be made by 28 February 2019

### Emerging Issues

- Cuckooing
- Suicide prevention

Note:

#### Cuckooing

This is where drug dealers target the homes of vulnerable people and use them as bases for drug dealing.
This is known as ‘cuckooing’.
The CSP has recognised that this is an emerging issue for Hertsmere.
Later in the month our officers are leading on a multi-agency workshop to enable them to put in place a Hertsmere specific partnership policy to ensure that victims, and potential victims, are safeguarded and that the perpetrators are dealt with appropriately.
Further work will be to train partners, raise awareness in the community and identify what support can be given to vulnerable residents within the borough.

#### Suicide Prevention

Just embarking on work with Network Rail, the Samaritans and British Transport Police to draw up a community action plan to cover awareness, bereavement support, media, information sharing etc

### Performance Management Arrangements in 2018/19

Note:

- 2020 Vision, Corporate Plan for Hertsmere published for the period 2017 - 2020

- Alongside the overarching plan:
  - A retrospective Annual Report which details the Council’s achievements against each priority area over the last year
  - A forward looking annual Action Plan which sets out the key areas of work under each Corporate priority for the year ahead.
- To support this, a new Performance Management Framework was introduced.
  In line with the plan it significantly reduced the number of PIs being reported corporately to ensure an increased focus on key services.
  15 Quality Service Indicators (QSIs) have been selected to reflect the core council services and are closely monitored to ensure high levels of service delivery.
  A wider set of Key Performance Indicators are reviewed by the Executive Performance Panel on an exception basis and Senior Officers maintain an overview across all 100 corporately collected PIs.

- Performance Management now takes place as follows:
  - Corporate Action Plan and Quality Service Indicators – quarterly updates to Chief Officers, Executive Performance Management Panel and Executive.
  - Key Performance Indicators – quarterly update to Chief Officers and by exception to Executive Performance Management Panel.
  - Management Information Indicators – quarterly update to Chief Officers
- Issues requiring further investigation are referred to the relevant Scrutiny Committee.

## IT

+++

### Business Support and Projects Team

Note:

- The team have managed major system upgrades of the Planning, Land Charges, Income Management and Finance systems. They have led on successful new system implementations of the Environmental Health, Licensing and PCI Pal (PCI compliance) systems.
- Phase one of moving the payment system to a fully hosted platform was completed. The final phases of this project will be completed in Q2/3 2019.
- The Business and Projects Team have developed good working relationships with all areas of the Council and are integral to the organisation providing a high level of customer service to our residents and businesses.
- The team have worked closely with colleagues in Planning to implement mobile apps on iPads for Planning officers. Work has started on implementing mobile apps on iPads for Enforcement officers. This work has supported to Planning in their move towards an agile workforce.
- The team are currently preparing for Council Tax billing, Housing Benefits and Financial year end processing. The team prides itself on maintaining minimal system downtime at year-end and when system upgrades are required throughout the year by working evenings and at weekends.

+++

### Infrastructure Team

Note:

- During the year the Infrastructure Team have migrated all systems running on older servers and storage units to a supplier-supported environment. Equipment and operating systems no longer supported by the supplier have been decommissioned. This is required to maintain our central government Public Services Network (PSN) compliance.
- The corporate Wi-Fi system was replaced in the summer giving an improved service to staff, Members and the public visiting the Civic offices.
- The team successfully completed a major project to replace Members iPads and to migrate Members and staff who used the Good app to Microsoft Office 365 cloud platform. The internal email server was also upgraded which will allow other staff to be migrated to MS365 in the future.
- As with the Business Support Team, the Infrastructure Team prides itself in keeping system downtime to a minimum by working evenings and weekends when applying essential system updates.

+++

### Service Desk Team

Note:

- The service desk team have supported the project to replace Members iPads and the move to MS365. The initial 2-year contract to support Hertfordshire Building Control ended in August but IDS are continuing support on a rolling monthly basis. The service desk team have continued to provide a high level of first level support to the organisation.
- A minor restructure of the team allowed the permanent appointment of a Service Desk Administrator and the recruitment of a second IDS Apprentice. The cost of the apprenticeship training is being funded from the apprenticeship levy. Both apprentices are Hertsmere residents and are a great asset to the team.

+++

### Technical Security

Note:

The threat of a cyber-attack is increasing with a number of high profile successful attacks in the media in the last year. The team have worked hard with limited resources to protect the Councils assets and reputation. Our cyber defences are continually reviewed and updated. The team monitor the Councils systems overnight and at weekends.
In a typical month:
The email filter blocks 130 Viruses.
28,500 Spams emails are rejected.
10,500 emails are quarantined for review by the infrastructure team.
475 cyber intrusion events occurred.

### Ongoing Projects

Note:

- Information and Digital Services are working with the Society of IT Managers (SOCITM) on a project to look at the feasibility of migrating current systems to a public cloud. This is a major project and one few councils have undertaken. A migration to the cloud could reduce costs, support agile working and free up property assets.
- Work has begun on replacing the desktop infrastructure. The current desktop PCs and laptops are now 6 years old. A refresh will give an opportunity for the organisation to look at new and different ways of working. IDS are working with Chief Officers, the Head of HR and Customer Services and Service Heads on finding the best solution for Hertsmere.
- Hertsmere have partnered with Oxford City Council and 11 other local authorities in a successful Local Digital funding bid for a discovery project to look at the use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and chatbots in local government. The four areas the project will research are Planning, Housing Benefits, Highways, Waste, and recycling. This exciting project could lead to further partnership working in the future.
- IDS have worked closely with the Payroll Manager in securing the contract to provide payroll services to Hertfordshire Building Control.
- As you can see, it has been a varied and busy year for IDS with all members of the team contributing and playing a full part in providing a professional IT support service to the organisation.
